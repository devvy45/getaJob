# Getajob reels

Turn supplied excerpts into a coherent, engaging career reel using the approved v7 direction. This is an editing workflow, not a fixed video template: adapt the story, crops, and visual emphasis to the material.

Read [the production script](references/production-script.md) before editing. It is the canonical creative brief and reusable instruction set. Read [the technical notes](references/technical-notes.md) when processing media, retiming words, or revising an approved export.

## Decisions already settled

- Keep the **complete approved hook wording**, normally within **2–3 seconds including any hold**. Do not silently shorten it. Write concise scripts before requesting new recordings. The approved pilot uses the full recording at 1.9× speed; that value is a reference, not an automatic rate for every new recording.
- Use the user's own voice for the hook and a useful transition question. The founder's voice carries the interview. Separate these voices rather than playing them over one another.
- Preserve the user's **original pitch**. Hook and transition share pacing, EQ, compression, and level treatment. Speed changes preserve pitch. No artificial deepening unless newly requested.
- Keep the website's lowercase **getajob** wordmark: DM Sans 600, tight tracking, **#FF9674**. No background pill, highlight, outline, or drop shadow. Use the supplied transparent wordmarks.
- Use cream editorial serif headlines, orange italic emphasis, and restrained sans-serif captions. Avoid bland, uniformly sized text and random travelling words.
- Keep the podcast visually present in the middle. Lists and narrated questions can sit on blurred, darkened moving podcast footage. Return smoothly to sharp footage. Explanatory numbers are **brief accents over the interview**, not long contrasting full-screen slides.
- Show one principal visual idea at a time. Do not duplicate a large callout with another subtitle or crowd the frame with simultaneous effects. Include short, purposeful breathing spaces.
- Synchronize words through all trims and speed changes. Follow the active speaker and gestures when reframing to 9:16; check late leans, not just opening frames.
- Use moderate noise cleanup, matched speech levels, a very quiet ducked background bed, and sparse effects. Keep intelligibility first.
- End promptly after the final useful sentence with a clean, roughly two-second branded frame: “Get better at getting hired. @getajob”.

## Work and revisions

Establish the story, exact hook, and need for user recordings before polishing. If clips alone supply a strong opening and segue, use them; do not manufacture extra narration. If supplied recordings are missing, prepare the actual scripts and continue independent editing work while requesting only the missing audio.

On revisions, change the requested section and preserve approved sections and mix. Keep numbered exports. Do not bring back rejected pitch changes, shortened hook wording, or long contrasting middle cards.

Use available tools rather than assuming the pilot's temporary runtime is installed. Follow the technical notes for original-pitch voice processing, source-word retiming, and reliable rendering. This skill supplies the creative and production workflow; it is not a standalone rendering application.

Verify the rendered export at phone scale. Check actual output timing and levels; distinguish speech-recognition checks from listening. Never claim to have heard a mix without audio access. Record any unperformed audition in the QA notes and continue the useful work that available tools support.

Deliver the MP4 and concise change notes. Include a cover and editable timing/project files when useful. Editing authorization does not imply publishing.
