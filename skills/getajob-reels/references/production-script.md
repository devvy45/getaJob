# Getajob reel production script

This is the final creative direction approved through the Immad Akhund pilot, ending at v7. Use these preferences instead of reconstructing the earlier rejected drafts. Apply current user instructions when they change the brief.

## Purpose and inputs

Create mobile Instagram marketing reels for getajob from supplied podcast fragments, YouTube excerpts, founder interviews, hiring-manager conversations, and practical career advice. The reel should earn attention with a useful idea and deliver its payoff, rather than decorate an arbitrary clip.

Work from the provided clips, their original audio, and any user voiceovers. For links, obtain the intended excerpt and context using available tools; ask for a local clip or timestamp only when needed. Identify the speaker, relevant claims, useful source ranges, footage quality, and rights/source information. Verify specific factual hook claims where necessary; never infer a valuation, hiring policy, or speaker identity from appearance.

Choose a coherent story before treating input order as the edit order. Remove redundant setup, distracting pauses, and filler selectively. Preserve the speaker's meaning, qualifications, and natural delivery. Do not assemble unrelated statements into an apparent continuous claim.

## Story and scene treatment

### 1. Hook: normally 2–3 seconds

Lead immediately with a specific, truthful promise or surprising takeaway. A dedicated getajob graphic with the user's voiceover is approved. No extra logo bumper before the hook.

Retain the **entire user-approved spoken hook**. A previous attempt to shorten the recording to only its final clause was rejected. Do not replace full wording because it is easier to fit the time budget. For future scripts, write a concise complete hook before the user records it; if completeness, clarity, and the requested duration genuinely conflict, surface that exact tradeoff rather than silently removing words.

The approved pilot's full hook is:

> The founder of a $5B startup shares the 5 traits he looks for in every hire.

Its supplied recording is accelerated at **1.9×**, with a short hold bringing the opening to **3.00 seconds**. This is the accepted rate for that recording, not a universal speed for unrelated narrators. For new recordings, compute the rate from the spoken excerpt and desired duration, then check intelligibility and synchronization.

Display the headline in a stable composition with cream serif type, large orange emphasis, and a clear size hierarchy. Words or short phrases reveal with narration; no random flying or bouncing. The key credibility cue and promised takeaway should be easy to scan.

### 2. Interview and useful lists

Give the original speaker the floor. Reframe landscape footage intentionally for vertical viewing, with enough room for eyes, mouth, head, gestures, and the microphone. Switch between a medium view and a modest punch-in when meaning warrants it. Avoid constant zooming, static bad crops, and cropping someone out when they lean.

A speaker does **not** have to remain sharp and unobstructed behind every list. For a sequence such as the five traits:

- Continue relevant podcast footage behind the text.
- Blur and darken it enough that cream/orange type remains readable, including over a white shirt or jacket.
- Reveal each list item when it is spoken. Previously revealed items may remain in cream while the current one receives orange emphasis.
- Keep the same brand type hierarchy and a stable layout.
- Allow a brief final hold for the complete list, without turning it into a long detached slide.

The pilot's fast trait list was slowed to **0.90×** with matching video and word timings. Slow only the dense passages that benefit from it, not the entire founder interview.

### 3. Narrated question / section break

Use a transition only when it adds clarity or curiosity. Prefer a natural segue when it works. Otherwise use a short user-recorded question, for example:

> How does he test for curiosity?

The user's question should have the **same perceived pace and voice treatment as the opening hook**, with the user's original pitch. The approved pilot uses **1.9×** for both. Do not leave the transition noticeably slower or weaker than the opening.

Place the question over blurred, darkened podcast footage. Let its word reveals follow the user's narration, then clear the blur into the live interview as the founder answers. Keep a small amount of breathing room; avoid simultaneous founder speech underneath the user's question.

### 4. Explanation and emphasis

Keep visual continuity with the podcast. Prefer short, useful accents such as **“An hour”** or **“45 minutes”** on the live interview, clearing them after roughly 1–1.5 seconds when the content permits. Long cream/charcoal explanation slides in the middle were rejected as too contrasting, even when the typography itself looked good.

A highlight owns the text layer while it is on screen: suppress duplicate ordinary subtitles rather than covering the same point twice. Return to normal captions afterwards. Use a longer diagram, cutaway, screenshot, or stock shot only when it genuinely explains something the interview image cannot. Relevant footage can support meaning, but do not fill a shot quota or present illustrative footage as factual evidence.

### 5. Payoff and ending

Keep the strongest practical insight or useful final sentence intact. Cut unnecessary trailing recording time. End promptly with a minimal branded frame lasting about **1.5–2 seconds**:

> Get better at getting hired.
> @getajob

Use cream serif text, orange italic emphasis, and the clean orange wordmark. A restrained fade is appropriate. Match the hook's design language. Include discreet source attribution without competing with the main message. Avoid an unrelated sales interruption or a long closing bumper.

## Voiceovers and audio

The founder's original voice is the main content. The user's recordings supply the editorial hook and transition where needed. Request these recordings when the edit needs them; do not assume a file has arrived, silently clone a voice, or invent a synthetic narrator.

For a new reel, provide the user with the exact concise hook and optional question scripts before recording. Preserve the supplied wording unless the user authorizes a rewrite. Once supplied, trim empty recording lead/tail while retaining complete first/last words and natural consonants.

**Original vocal pitch is the approved preference.** The artificial lower-pitch treatment tested in earlier versions was explicitly reversed. Speed up the user's hook and question with pitch preserved. Match both voiceovers in EQ, compression, and apparent level; the question must not sound like an untreated afterthought. Modest clarity EQ, compression, level matching, and limiting are approved; artificial deepening, heavy echo, and dramatic voice effects are not defaults.

Apply moderate noise reduction and remove rumble/hiss without aggressively gating soft words or producing metallic speech. Balance different source clips before the final mix. Keep noise cleanup separate from claims that all noise has been removed.

A very quiet, warm ambient music bed is approved. Duck it under speech, avoid masking the voice, and fade it in/out. Sparse, low-level impacts or transition sounds can help; no sound on every word and no dense stack of whooshes. Use original or appropriately licensed audio and retain source information.

The pilot used an original warm pad around **−34 dBFS RMS before ducking**, reduced beneath active speech; this is a starting point, not a loudness target for every music file. Dialogue stays clearly dominant. A final mix around **−16 LUFS integrated**, with roughly **−1.8 dBTP** headroom before AAC, is a useful starting target. Inspect actual post-encode measurements; do not confuse a requested normalization target with measured output.

## Subtitles and synchronization

Transcribe with word timestamps. Check names, numbers, negations, jargon, and low-confidence words against the actual audio or an authoritative source. In the pilot, ASR misheard **product-minded** as **partner-minded**; the original episode transcript supplied the correction.

Use readable phone-scale captions, typically one or two short lines. The pilot used approximately **76 px DM Sans at 1080×1920**, with larger serif/italic emphasis around key phrases. These are layout starting points; fit the actual words rather than shrinking everything until it technically fits.

Words reveal in place as spoken. Keep the composition stable and the hierarchy clear. Retiming must account for **every cut, removed pause, speed change, and new section duration**. At source time `s`, for a clip beginning at `a`, starting in the output at `o`, played at rate `r`:

`output_time = o + (s − a) / r`

Apply the same rate to video when lips are visible. Recompute word times after speed changes; do not slow speech to conceal inaccurate subtitles. Handle compound words sensibly and keep captions out of the right-hand controls and bottom account/caption area. Check the rendered output rather than trusting the timeline alone.

ASR timings are estimates. Compare important word onsets again after processing and inspect synchronization at phrase boundaries. Do not promise “100% verified” from automatic transcription alone.

## Revision and delivery rules

Preserve approved sections when a revision is local. For example, a request to change the question's speed does not reopen the hook, branding, crop choices, or music mix. Retain source/segment maps and numbered versions so approved content can be reused reliably.

Default export: **1080×1920, 9:16, 30 fps, H.264/AAC MP4**, with fast-start playback. Adapt frame rate when the source or user needs it; 30 fps is the tested pilot default, not an Instagram rule. The approved reel happened to be **40.4 seconds**; duration for another story is content-dependent.

Verify the full render: opening, all text states, faces/late leans, muted readability, transitions, final sentence, ending duration, frame count, audio duration, loudness, clipping, and sync. Use headphones/phone listening when available. If audio cannot be auditioned, be honest: use transcription and numeric checks, record that limitation in QA notes, and do not claim to have heard the mix.

Deliver a clickable review MP4 with brief change notes. Provide the cover, timing/project files, and source/license notes when useful. Do not publish or upload to Instagram solely because the reel is approved for editing.
